# Writing a Paper Deck

## Titles

Write the sentence the speaker wants the audience to believe, not the category of the slide.

Weak: `Validation metrics`

Strong: `Accuracy hides the errors that matter`

Weak: `Architecture`

Strong: `The request crosses one boundary, not five`

## Compression

Cut throat-clearing words such as "basically", "in order to", and "it is important to note". Prefer verbs over abstract nouns. If a card needs punctuation to hold multiple clauses, split it.

## Rhythm

Alternate density:

- a bold claim
- a concrete diagram
- a question
- a mechanism
- an example

Do not repeat the same three-card layout slide after slide. Repetition should create a motif, not expose a template.

## Evidence

Use real numbers, dates, names, screenshots, or examples when available. Do not invent evidence for visual completeness. Mark estimates clearly.

## Closing

End on a synthesis, decision, or next action. Avoid "Thank you" as the sole final slide. The final sentence should be worth photographing or repeating.
